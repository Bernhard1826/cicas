%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This paper studies the end-to-end automation from PKI specifications to
compliance-checking code; the central question is how, lacking rule-by-rule
manual ground truth, to place normative rules, the lintability boundary,
generated code, and verification evidence on a single re-checkable chain.
Experiments show that only a portion of the genuine rules in RFC~5280 and CABF BR
can be reduced to single-certificate static lints; among these rules, existing
zlint still has a quantifiable cognate coverage gap, and our system can generate
a batch of compilable, auditable candidate lints for the uncovered rules.

The most important conclusion is negative: \CodeEqIR{} and \CodeEqSpec{} must not
be conflated. Certificate-level execution verification can determinize IR-level
faithfulness on the certifiable subset, but whether the final code expresses the
specification itself still needs independent synonymy judgment and execution
evidence. External validation covers the two halves of this chain separately:
the zlint manual gold standard checks rule extraction and lintability
determination, and execution on real certificates checks the structural hits of
the emitted lints. Hence our methodological stance: determinize every link in
the verification chain that can be determinized, while honestly disclosing the
boundaries that cannot be reduced, cannot be ablated, or still depend on a
semantic judge.
