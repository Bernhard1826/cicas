\section*{Ethical Considerations}
This work analyzes only publicly available material: the text of open PKI
specifications (RFC~5280, the CA/Browser Forum Baseline Requirements) and
publicly observable certificates (TLS handshakes to the Tranco top-1M domains
and samples drawn from public Certificate Transparency logs). No private data,
credentials, or human subjects are involved, and no live system is probed or
attacked. The generated lints are analysis artifacts run offline against
certificate bytes; the paper deliberately reports structural findings without
overstating them as ecosystem-wide misissuance rates, and every
certificate-level finding is gated behind an independent structural audit
before being reported. We see no foreseeable harm from releasing the method,
the generated checks, or the aggregate statistics.

\section*{Open Science}
In line with the venue's open-science policy, we will make available the
artifacts needed to reproduce our results: the specification-to-IR extraction
pipeline, the frozen typed vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$ and the finite
atomic-template set $\mathcal{A}$, the deterministic renderer and mechanical
translator, the generated cicasgen\_* lints, the coverage-alignment
and synonymy-judgment harnesses, and the evaluation corpora manifests (the
zlint maintainer scope-mapping sheets and the certificate hashes used for the
Tranco/CT scans). Deterministic components (recall conservation, lintability
gating, coverage aggregation, certificate-level execution verification) can be
recomputed exactly from the released inputs.
