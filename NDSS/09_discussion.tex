\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Applicability Across PKIs}
\label{subsec:applicability}

The framework's core mechanism is not specific to PKI. Layer~1's RFC~2119
keyword set can be reconfigured to another ecosystem's deontic keywords such as
ISO's shall/should/may. The method applies to specification sources that jointly
satisfy three conditions. First, they express mandatory or recommended levels
with recognizable formal deontic keywords. Second, the constrained target is a
structured, machine-parsable artifact such as a certificate, protocol message, or
configuration file. Third, they have a hierarchical section structure that
supports a knowledge graph and scope inheritance. This gives the methodology
value beyond PKI alone.

\subsection{Recommendations for Specification Authors and Tool Maintainers}
\label{subsec:recommendations}

For specification authors, three drafting principles reduce extraction ambiguity.
They are one obligation per sentence, referring to fields precisely by ASN.1 path
rather than pronoun, and normalizing cross-document references with a fixed
pattern. For lint maintainers, we suggest writing the description directly as a
code\_summary that explicitly gives the field path, assertion type, value, and
severity and cites the specification clause. We find that many lint descriptions
are neither specification citations nor faithful implementation summaries, which
forces the coverage analysis to first compute a code\_summary before aligning and
adds overhead and adjudication noise.
