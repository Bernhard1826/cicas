\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

We did not identify a comparable automated baseline for a head-to-head
comparison. To our knowledge, no prior end-to-end system extracts Web PKI
issuance rules from natural-language standards, classifies their lintability,
and synthesizes lint checks with explicit audit evidence on the same input.

The accuracy of lintability classification, code-generation rate, and synonymy
rate all depend on IR accuracy. SAIV audits and repairs IR outputs over multiple
rounds, but the present evaluation does not separately quantify Layer~2
extraction error. Improving IR extraction and measuring this error therefore
remain important future work. We also do not measure run-to-run agreement of Layer~2 extraction
or compare the pipeline with direct end-to-end LLM baselines. Accordingly, the
study does not establish a comparative stability claim or the reproducibility of
LLM-based extraction.

DLEP's core mechanism is not inherently specific to PKI standards, but this
study does not evaluate transfer to another standards ecosystem. Layer~1
currently targets RFC~2119 keywords such as MUST, SHALL, SHOULD, and MAY. Its
regular expressions could be adapted to other formal deontic vocabularies, such
as the ISO ``shall''/``should''/``may'' triple and equivalent forms in other
languages. Such transfer would require three properties: (1) recognizable formal
deontic keywords; (2) structured, machine-parsable constraint targets, such as
certificates, protocol messages, or configuration files; and (3) a document
hierarchy that supports graph construction and scope-inheritance modeling.

Code generation also depends on the regularity of the target lint framework.
It works for zlint because many checks are small predicates over a parsed
certificate and can be represented as typed DSL trees. Lints with idiosyncratic
control flow, global state, multi-artifact reasoning, or semantics outside
reusable atoms require DSL extensions. NON\_GENERIC atoms make this boundary
concrete: they improve coverage but encode hand-curated domain knowledge. A new
ecosystem would need specialized atoms, renderers, and validation tests before
similar generation rates could be expected.

\subsection{Recommendations for Specification Authors and Tool Maintainers}
\label{subsec:recommendations}

The methodology and empirical findings support recommendations for PKI
specification authors and linting-tool maintainers.

For PKI specification authors, we suggest three drafting principles. First,
each sentence should express at most one normative obligation; multiple
obligations should be separately numbered rather than combined through
subordinate clauses. Second, field references should identify the constrained
certificate field explicitly, where possible using ASN.1-derived paths such as
tbsCertificate.\allowbreak extensions.\allowbreak subjectAltName.\allowbreak
dNSName rather than vague terms such as ``the SAN extension'' or pronouns such
as ``it''. Third, cross-document references should use a canonical form such as
``[verb] [document] Section~[section] (with step or parameter where applicable)''
rather than vague forms such as ``as described in [RFC~X]'' or ``see
[RFC~X]''. This form makes each reference easier to map to a typed graph edge.

For maintainers of static certificate linters, we recommend an
implementation-aligned code\_summary in the metadata description: one
RFC~2119-style sentence that states the field path, assertion type, allowed or
forbidden values, lint severity, and citation. zlint's
w\_ct\_sct\_policy\_count\_unsatisfied, for instance, illustrates why a metadata
description alone can be insufficient: it is described only as ``Check if
certificate has enough embedded SCTs to meet Apple CT Policy'' and does not
itself provide a source citation or a precise implementation summary. For this
reason, our coverage analysis uses the Stage~0 code-summarization step in
Algorithm~1 before a normative rule can be aligned with lint behavior at the IR
level. This step adds engineering overhead and can introduce adjudication noise
at the partial/none boundary.
